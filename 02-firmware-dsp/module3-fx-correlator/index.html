    Module 3: Real-Time FX Correlator Engine & Visibility Telemetry Streamer¶

    1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation¶

    1.1. Executive Summary & High-Level Objectives¶

    Module 3 constitutes the primary digital signal processing (DSP) engine and telemetry backbone of the decametric solar radio interferometer backend. Situated directly downstream of the multi-process ingestion and coarse time-alignment stages established in Module 1 and Module 2, Module 3 operates on twin continuous baseband in-phase and quadrature (\(I/Q\)) data streams at a sampling rate of \(f_s = 10.0\text{ MSPS}\) (\(T_s = 100\text{ ns}\)). The combined aggregate input throughput across two POSIX shared memory ring buffers reaches \(80\text{ MB/s}\). The architectural mandate of Module 3 is to transform these raw, uncorrelated baseband voltage time series into calibrated, cross-correlated complex visibilities, compute power spectra in real time, and dispatch the integrated radiometric telemetry to remote visualization stations.

    The subsystem is architected as an FX Correlator comprising the following computational pipelines: 1. F-Engine (Frequency Decomposition): A windowed Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) subsystem implemented via FFTW3 with single-precision floating-point arithmetic and ARM NEON SIMD vector extensions. It decomposes the \(10.0\text{ MHz}\) digitized bandwidth into \(N = 2048\) discrete spectral channels, achieving a fine spectral resolution of \(\Delta f \approx 4.8828\text{ kHz}\). 2. X-Engine (Cross-Multiplication & Calibration): A vectorized Cartesian cross-multiplication pipeline computing the complex instantaneous cross-power spectrum \(S_{12}[m]\), accompanied by simultaneous auto-power spectra \(S_{11}[m]\) and \(S_{22}[m]\). It incorporates sub-sample phase correction mechanisms for geometric fringe stopping and instrumental bandpass phase flattening. 3. Vector Accumulator (Welch Integration): A real-time time-integration engine operating with a 50% overlapping sliding window (hop size \(L = 1024\) samples). It coherently and radiometrically averages consecutive spectral frames across an integration interval \(\tau \approx 200\text{ ms}\) (\(M = 1953\) frames), suppressing uncorrelated Gaussian thermal noise by a factor of \(\sqrt{M}\) and compressing data throughput from \(80\text{ MB/s}\) to \(\sim 41.1\text{ KB/s}\). 4. Visibility Telemetry Streamer: A zero-copy, non-blocking POSIX UDP socket interface encapsulating the integrated power spectra into 8224-byte flat binary datagrams dispatched across local area networks to the visualizer client (viewer.py).

    The design targets four quantitative technical objectives: * Real-Time Continuous DSP Throughput: Continuous ingestion of dual 10.0 MSPS streams (\(80\text{ MB/s}\)) on a single ARM Cortex-A76 core of a Raspberry Pi 5 without sample drops or buffer overflows[cite: 1, 4]. * Fine Spectral & Temporal Resolution: Channel bandwidth \(\Delta f = 4.8828\text{ kHz}\) across \(N = 2048\) channels and time cadence \(\tau = 200\text{ ms}\) (\(M = 1953\) frames), optimized for tracking rapid solar radio burst frequency drifts[cite: 1, 4]. * Coherent Signal Detection & Radiometric Gain: Radiometric thermal noise suppression scaling as \(\sqrt{M}\) (\(> 14.9\text{ dB}\) improvement over single FFT frames, \(> 30\text{ dB}\) over raw samples), maintaining spatial coherence \(\gamma \ge 0.95\) on zero-baseline benchtop tests[cite: 1, 4]. * Low-Latency Telemetry Transport: Dispatch of packed 8224-byte UDP binary packets within \(1.6\text{ ms}\) after integration completion, with zero network blocking on the DSP core[cite: 1, 4].

    1.2. Problem Formulation: Beyond Coarse Alignment to Astronomical Fringe Synthesis¶

    1.2.1. Residual Sub-Sample Delay and Continuous Geometric Delay (\(\tau_g(t)\))¶

    Module 2 addressed the gross asynchronous startup latency inherent to independent USB 3.0 controller enumeration by applying time-domain cross-correlation over snapshots of length \(N = 65536\) samples[cite: 1, 2, 9]. By executing a buffer skip operation (skip(k_offset)), Module 2 forced the discrete sample skew \(k_0 \in \mathbb{Z}\) to zero (\(|k| \le 1\text{ sample}\), or \(\le 100\text{ ns}\))[cite: 1, 2, 9]. However, this coarse alignment operates strictly at integer multiples of the sampling period \(T_s = 100\text{ ns}\)[cite: 1, 3, 7].

    In astronomical radio interferometry, two outdoor antenna elements separated by an East-West baseline vector \(\mathbf{B}\) intercept celestial wavefronts arriving from an astronomical source (such as the Sun) at zenith angle \(\theta(t)\)[cite: 1, 7, 8]. The physical wavefront encounters a continuous path difference, creating an astronomical geometric delay[cite: 1, 7, 8]: $\(\tau_g(t) = \frac{\mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{s}(t)}{c} = \frac{B \sin\theta(t)}{c} \tag{1}\)$ where \(c \approx 2.99792 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}\) is the speed of light in vacuum, \(B\) is the baseline baseline distance (\(10\text{ m} \le B \le 20\text{ m}\)), and \(\mathbf{s}(t)\) is the unit vector pointing toward the source[cite: 1, 7, 8].

    Because the Earth rotates with diurnal angular velocity \(\omega_\oplus \approx 7.2921 \times 10^{-5}\text{ rad/s}\), the zenith angle \(\theta(t)\) and geometric delay \(\tau_g(t)\) are continuous real-valued functions of time[cite: 1, 7, 8]. The total time delay differential between the two receiver inputs is[cite: 7]: $\(\tau_{\text{total}}(t) = \tau_g(t) + \tau_{\text{inst}} + \Delta\tau_{\text{res}} \tag{2}\)$ where \(\tau_{\text{inst}}\) represents differential electrical delay along the RF feedlines and filters, and \(\Delta\tau_{\text{res}} \in (-T_s/2, +T_s/2)\) represents the residual uncompensated sub-sample delay remaining after Module 2's coarse integer sample alignment[cite: 1, 7]. By the Fourier time-shift property, a continuous time delay \(\Delta\tau\) manifests across the frequency domain as a linear phase slope[cite: 1, 7, 14]: $\(\frac{d(\Delta\phi)}{df} = 2\pi \Delta\tau \tag{3}\)$ Because \(\Delta\tau\) is fractional and time-varying, it cannot be eliminated by shifting discrete pointers in time-domain ring buffers[cite: 1, 3, 7].

    1.2.2. Spectral Channelization vs. Fringe Washing (Decorrelation)¶

    If two broadband signals exhibiting delay differential \(\tau\) are directly multiplied across the entire receiver bandwidth \(B_{\text{RF}} = 8.0\text{ MHz}\) without spectral decomposition, the resulting cross-correlation response suffers from severe decorrelation (commonly known as fringe washing)[cite: 1, 7, 8]. The integrated cross-power response across a continuous passband \([f_0 - B_{\text{RF}}/2, f_0 + B_{\text{RF}}/2]\) scales according to[cite: 1, 7, 8]: $\(R_{12}(\tau) = \int_{f_0 - B_{\text{RF}}/2}^{f_0 + B_{\text{RF}}/2} S(f) e^{j 2\pi f \tau} df = S_0 B_{\text{RF}} \cdot \text{sinc}(B_{\text{RF}} \tau) \cdot e^{j 2\pi f_0 \tau} \tag{4}\)$ where \(\text{sinc}(x) = \frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}\)[cite: 8]. For a full \(8.0\text{ MHz}\) analog bandwidth, the fringe pattern envelope contracts to a narrow width of \(\Delta\tau_{\text{null}} = \frac{1}{B_{\text{RF}}} = \frac{1}{8 \times 10^6\text{ Hz}} = 125\text{ ns}\)[cite: 1, 8]. On an interferometric baseline of \(B = 20\text{ m}\), the geometric delay varies over a range of \(\pm \frac{20\text{ m}}{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}} \approx \pm 66.7\text{ ns}\)[cite: 1, 7]. If uncompensated, a fractional error of just \(60\text{ ns}\) attenuates the cross-correlation amplitude by more than \(50\%\) (\(\text{sinc}(8.0 \times 10^6 \times 60 \times 10^{-9}) \approx 0.68\))[cite: 1, 8].

    The F-Engine overcomes fringe washing through narrow-band channelization[cite: 1, 7, 8]. By subdividing the digitized bandwidth into \(N = 2048\) discrete channels, each channel occupies a narrow spectral slice[cite: 1, 4]: $\(\Delta f = \frac{f_s}{N} = \frac{10.0\text{ MHz}}{2048} \approx 4882.8125\text{ Hz} \tag{5}\)$ Within any individual spectral channel \(m\), the phase dispersion across the bin is restricted to[cite: 1, 7, 8]: $\(\Delta\Phi_{\text{bin}} = 2\pi \Delta f \tau_g \le 2\pi \times (4.8828 \times 10^3\text{ Hz}) \times (66.7 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s}) \approx 2.05 \times 10^{-3}\text{ rad} \approx 0.117^\circ \tag{6}\)$ The fringe decorrelation factor within the bin evaluates to[cite: 1, 7, 8]: $\(\eta_{\text{fringe}} = \text{sinc}(\Delta f \tau_g) = \frac{\sin(\pi \times 4882.8 \times 66.7 \times 10^{-9})}{\pi \times 4882.8 \times 66.7 \times 10^{-9}} \ge 0.99983 \tag{7}\)$ This demonstrates that intra-bin decorrelation loss is strictly below \(0.02\%\) across the entire decametric observation band for baselines up to \(B \le 20\text{ m}\)[cite: 1, 7, 8]. Consequently, the interferometer can operate in both phase-tracked mode (fringe stopping) and uncompensated transit mode without loss of fringe visibility[cite: 1, 7, 8].

    1.2.3. Instrumental Bandpass Phase Dispersion (\(\Delta\phi_0[m]\))¶

    In physical receiver hardware, the two analog signal chains exhibit independent transfer functions[cite: 1, 7, 12]: $\(H_1(f) = |H_1(f)| e^{j \phi_1(f)}, \quad H_2(f) = |H_2(f)| e^{j \phi_2(f)} \tag{8}\)$ These functions are shaped by analog intermediate frequency (IF) bandpass filters (\(8.0\text{ MHz}\) nominal bandwidth), low-noise amplifiers (LNAs), bias tees, and coaxial interconnects[cite: 1, 7, 12]. Due to manufacturing tolerances and component temperature coefficients, the differential group delay \(\tau_g(f) = -\frac{1}{2\pi} \frac{d\phi}{df}\) is non-uniform across the passband[cite: 1, 7]. This induces a static, frequency-dependent instrumental phase error[cite: 1, 7]: $\(\Delta\phi_0[m] = \phi_1(f_m) - \phi_2(f_m) \tag{9}\)$ Without measuring and applying this calibration vector, the reconstructed complex visibility phase will be warped, introducing artificial phase slopes and distorting the measured wavefront angles[cite: 1, 7, 14].

    3. Theoretical & Mathematical Foundations¶

    3.1. Mathematical Signal Model in Frequency Domain¶

    Let the baseband voltage signals from the two receiver front-ends, following coarse alignment (\(k = 0\)), be modeled as complex discrete-time stochastic processes[cite: 1, 4, 7]: $\(x_1[n] = s_1[n] + w_1[n], \quad x_2[n] = s_2[n] + w_2[n] \tag{10}\)$ where \(w_1[n], w_2[n] \sim \mathcal{CN}(0, \sigma_w^2)\) represent independent, identically distributed circular complex Gaussian thermal noise processes generated by receiver LNAs and ADCs[cite: 1, 7].

    In an interferometric deployment, the signals incident on the antennas contain contributions from the localized solar burst source \(s_{\text{sun}}(t)\), diffuse galactic synchrotron background radiation \(s_{\text{gal}}(t)\), and terrestrial radio frequency interference \(\text{rfi}(t)\)[cite: 1, 7, 12]. Passing these signals through the F-Engine channelizer yields the spectral representations at frequency bin \(m\) and time epoch \(t\)[cite: 1, 7, 12]: $\(X_1[m, t] = H_1[m] \Big( S_{\text{sun}}[m, t] + S_{\text{gal}, 1}[m, t] \Big) + \text{RFI}_1[m, t] + W_1[m, t] \tag{11}\)$ $\(X_2[m, t] = H_2[m] \Big( S_{\text{sun}}[m, t] e^{-j 2\pi f_m \tau_g(t)} + S_{\text{gal}, 2}[m, t] \Big) + \text{RFI}_2[m, t] + W_2[m, t] \tag{12}\)$ Here: * \(S_{\text{sun}}[m, t] \in \mathbb{C}\) is the complex analytic spectral amplitude of the solar emission[cite: 1, 7]. * \(f_m\) is the physical center frequency of bin \(m\)[cite: 1, 4, 7]. * \(\tau_g(t)\) is the geometric time delay between antennas along baseline \(\mathbf{B}\)[cite: 1, 7, 8]. * \(S_{\text{gal}, 1}, S_{\text{gal}, 2}\) are diffuse galactic noise components[cite: 1, 7, 12]. Because galactic emission is spatially extended across the hemisphere, it decorrelates on long baselines, yielding \(\langle S_{\text{gal}, 1} S_{\text{gal}, 2}^* \rangle \approx 0\)[cite: 7, 8, 12]. * \(W_1[m, t], W_2[m, t]\) are zero-mean Gaussian spectral noise variables satisfying \(\mathbb{E}[W_1 W_2^*] = 0\)[cite: 1, 7]. * \(H_1[m], H_2[m]\) represent the complex instrumental frequency response of channels 1 and 2[cite: 1, 7].

    3.2. F-Engine Mechanics: Discrete Windowing & FFTW3 Spectral Decomposition¶

    3.2.1. Windowing and Spectral Leakage Suppression (Symmetric Hanning)¶

    Truncating continuous data into finite length blocks of size \(N = 2048\) is equivalent to applying a rectangular window, whose frequency response exhibits large sidelobes (first sidelobe at \(-13.2\text{ dB}\))[cite: 1]. In decametric observations, strong terrestrial transmitters (such as high-frequency broadcast stations) can leak across dozens of adjacent bins, obscuring weak astronomical solar bursts[cite: 1, 7].

    To suppress spectral leakage, an analytic symmetric Hanning window \(w[n]\) is applied to each block prior to Fourier transformation[cite: 1, 4]: $\(w[n] = 0.5 \left[ 1 - \cos\left( \frac{2\pi n}{N - 1} \right) \right] = \sin^2\left( \frac{\pi n}{N - 1} \right), \quad n \in [0, N-1] \tag{13}\)$ The Hanning window suppresses the first sidelobe to \(-31.5\text{ dB}\) and accelerates asymptotic sidelobe roll-off to \(-18\text{ dB/octave}\) (compared to \(-6\text{ dB/octave}\) for rectangular windows)[cite: 1].

    Windowing reduces total signal energy, requiring compensation via deterministic normalizations[cite: 1]: 1. Coherent Amplitude Normalization Factor (\(S_1\)): $\(S_1 = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} w[n] = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \left[ 1 - \cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{N-1}\right) \right] = \frac{N}{2} = 1024 \tag{14}\)$ 2. Equivalent Noise Energy Normalization Factor (\(S_2\)): $\(S_2 = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} w^2[n] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \frac{1}{4} \left[ 1 - 2\cos\left(\frac{2\pi n}{N-1}\right) + \cos^2\left(\frac{2\pi n}{N-1}\right) \right] \tag{15}\)$ Using the identity \(\cos^2(\theta) = \frac{1 + \cos(2\theta)}{2}\): $\(S_2 = \frac{1}{4} \left[ N - 0 + \frac{N}{2} \right] = \frac{3}{8} N = \frac{3}{8} \times 2048 = 768.0 \tag{16}\)$ \(S_2\) is applied downstream during Welch accumulation to ensure unscaled power estimates[cite: 1, 10].

    3.2.2. Discrete Fourier Transform Implementation¶

    The windowed sequences \(\tilde{x}_1[n] = x_1[n] \cdot w[n]\) and \(\tilde{x}_2[n] = x_2[n] \cdot w[n]\) are transformed to the frequency domain via a 1D forward complex-to-complex DFT[cite: 1, 4]: $\(X[m] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \tilde{x}[n] \cdot e^{-j \frac{2\pi m n}{N}}, \quad m \in [0, N-1] \tag{17}\)$ With sampling frequency \(f_s = 10.0\text{ MSPS}\) and \(N = 2048\), the channel spacing is[cite: 1, 4]: $\(\Delta f = \frac{f_s}{N} = \frac{10.0 \times 10^6\text{ Hz}}{2048} = 4882.8125\text{ Hz} \tag{18}\)$ Following two-sided to centered frequency shifting (FFT Shift), the physical radio frequency corresponding to channel index \(m \in [0, 2047]\) with center frequency \(f_{\text{LO}} = 35.0\text{ MHz}\) is[cite: 1, 4]: $\(f_m = f_{\text{LO}} + \left( m - \frac{N}{2} \right) \Delta f = 35.0\text{ MHz} + (m - 1024) \times 4.8828125\text{ kHz} \tag{19}\)$ * Channel \(0\): \(f_0 = 35.0\text{ MHz} - 1024 \times 4882.8125\text{ Hz} = 30.000000\text{ MHz}\)[cite: 1, 5] * Channel \(1024\): \(f_{1024} = 35.000000\text{ MHz}\) (Direct Conversion DC / LO center)[cite: 1, 5] * Channel \(2047\): \(f_{2047} = 35.0\text{ MHz} + 1023 \times 4882.8125\text{ Hz} = 39.995117\text{ MHz}\)[cite: 1, 5]

    3.3. X-Engine Mechanics: Cross-Multiplication, Auto-Power & Fringe Rotation¶

    3.3.1. Complex Cross-Power Spectrum (\(S_{12}[m]\))¶

    At each frequency bin \(m\), the X-Engine computes the instantaneous complex cross-power spectrum by multiplying the complex spectrum of Channel 1 with the complex conjugate of Channel 2[cite: 1, 4]: $\(S_{12}[m] = X_1[m] \cdot X_2^*[m] \tag{20}\)$ Let \(X_1[m] = R_1[m] + j I_1[m]\) and \(X_2[m] = R_2[m] + j I_2[m]\), where \(R, I \in \mathbb{R}\)[cite: 1, 4]. The algebraic expansion is formulated as[cite: 1, 4]: $\(S_{12}[m] = (R_1 + j I_1)(R_2 - j I_2) = (R_1 R_2 + I_1 I_2) + j (I_1 R_2 - R_1 I_2) \tag{21}\)$ $\(\text{Re}(S_{12}[m]) = R_1[m] R_2[m] + I_1[m] I_2[m] \tag{22}\)$ $\(\text{Im}(S_{12}[m]) = I_1[m] R_2[m] - R_1[m] I_2[m] \tag{23}\)$ Decoupling into real scalar operations allows the C++ compiler to optimize execution using ARM NEON vector instructions (FMLA, FMLS) without pointer aliasing or complex-number overhead[cite: 1, 4, 10].

    3.3.2. Auto-Power Spectra (\(S_{11}[m]\), \(S_{22}[m]\))¶

    Simultaneously, the X-Engine evaluates the real-valued auto-power spectra of both antennas to monitor channel integrity and enable downstream coherence normalization[cite: 1, 4]: $\(S_{11}[m] = \vert{}X_1[m]\vert{}^2 = X_1[m] X_1^*[m] = R_1^2[m] + I_1^2[m] \tag{24}\)$ $\(S_{22}[m] = \vert{}X_2[m]\vert{}^2 = X_2[m] X_2^*[m] = R_2^2[m] + I_2^2[m] \tag{25}\)$

    3.3.3. Fringe Stopping & Phase Calibration API¶

    To track astronomical sources across the sky and compensate for instrumental cable mismatches, the complex cross-power spectrum is corrected by applying an analytic phase rotation[cite: 1, 4]: $\(S_{12, \text{corrected}}[m] = S_{12}[m] \cdot e^{-j \Phi[m, t]} \tag{26}\)$ The composite phase correction angle \(\Phi[m, t]\) decomposes into two terms[cite: 1, 4]: $\(\Phi[m, t] = \Delta\phi_0[m] + 2\pi f_m \tau_g(t) \tag{27}\)$ where \(\Delta\phi_0[m]\) is the static calibration phase vector, and \(2\pi f_m \tau_g(t)\) is the geometric fringe stopping term[cite: 1, 4].

    Expanding \(e^{-j \Phi} = \cos\Phi - j \sin\Phi\), the corrected real and imaginary visibility components evaluate to[cite: 1, 4]: $\(\text{Re}(S_{12, \text{corr}}) = \text{Re}(S_{12}) \cos\Phi + \text{Im}(S_{12}) \sin\Phi \tag{28}\)$ $\(\text{Im}(S_{12, \text{corr}}) = \text{Im}(S_{12}) \cos\Phi - \text{Re}(S_{12}) \sin\Phi \tag{29}\)$

    The engine supports two operational observation modes[cite: 1, 7]: * Fringe Stopping Mode: The phase correction \(\Phi[m, t]\) is evaluated dynamically at every integration epoch using ephemeris coordinates for the Sun, locking the interferometer's synthetic fringe pattern to the source[cite: 1, 7]. * Transit Mode: Setting \(\tau_g(t) = 0\) leaves the geometric phase uncompensated, allowing the Sun to drift through the fixed primary antenna beam pattern and generating sinusoidal fringe oscillations[cite: 1, 7, 8]. The transit fringe period \(T_{\text{fringe}}\) is given by[cite: 2, 8]: $\(T_{\text{fringe}} = \frac{\lambda}{B \omega_\oplus \cos\delta} = \frac{c}{f_0 B \omega_\oplus \cos\delta} \tag{30}\)$ For \(f_0 = 35.0\text{ MHz}\) (\(\lambda \approx 8.565\text{ m}\)), an East-West baseline \(B = 15.0\text{ m}\), and solar declination \(\delta \approx 0^\circ\) (equinox): $\(T_{\text{fringe}} = \frac{8.565\text{ m}}{15.0\text{ m} \times (7.2921 \times 10^{-5}\text{ rad/s}) \times 1.0} \approx 7830.4\text{ s} \approx 130.5\text{ min} \tag{31}\)$

    3.4. Time Integration (Welch's Method) & Statistical Radiometric Gain¶

    3.4.1. 50% Overlap Welch Accumulation¶

    To achieve consistent spectral estimation, Module 3 implements Welch's Method of Averaged Modified Periodograms using a 50% overlap[cite: 1, 4, 10]. Consecutive FFT blocks advance by hop size \(L = 1024\) samples[cite: 1, 4, 10]. The number of overlapping frames \(M\) integrated within an interval \(\tau = 0.200\text{ s}\) (\(200\text{ ms}\)) at \(f_s = 10.0\text{ MSPS}\) is calculated as[cite: 1, 4, 10]: $\(M = \left\lfloor \frac{f_s \cdot \tau}{L} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{10.0 \times 10^6 \times 0.200}{1024} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{2,000,000}{1024} \right\rfloor = 1953\text{ frames} \tag{32}\)$ The time-averaged cross-power spectral estimator is[cite: 1, 4]: $\(\langle S_{12}[m] \rangle = \frac{1}{M \cdot S_2} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} S_{12, k}[m] \tag{33}\)$ $\(\langle S_{11}[m] \rangle = \frac{1}{M \cdot S_2} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} S_{11, k}[m], \quad \langle S_{22}[m] \rangle = \frac{1}{M \cdot S_2} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} S_{22, k}[m] \tag{34}\)$ where \(S_2 = 768.0\) is the window energy normalization factor derived in Equation (16)[cite: 1, 10].

    3.4.2. Noise Suppression and Radiometer Sensitivity Equation¶

    Expanding the accumulated cross-power spectrum in terms of signal and noise components yields[cite: 1, 7]: $\(\langle S_{12}[m] \rangle = \frac{1}{M S_2} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} \Big[ S_1[m, k] S_2^*[m, k] + S_1[m, k] W_2^*[m, k] + W_1[m, k] S_2^*[m, k] + W_1[m, k] W_2^*[m, k] \Big] \tag{35}\)$ Because \(W_1\) and \(W_2\) originate from independent receiver hardware chains, their cross-products have zero expectation[cite: 1, 7]: $\(\mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{1}{M} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} W_1[m, k] W_2^*[m, k] \right] = 0 \tag{36}\)$ The variance of this accumulated noise sum decreases inversely with \(M\)[cite: 1, 7]: $\(\operatorname{Var}\left( \frac{1}{M} \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} W_1 W_2^* \right) = \frac{\sigma_{w1}^2 \sigma_{w2}^2}{M} \implies \sigma_{\text{cross}} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}} \tag{37}\)$

    According to the Ideal Radiometer Equation, the minimum detectable cross-correlated flux density (or equivalent system temperature sensitivity) scales as[cite: 1, 8]: $\(\Delta T_{\text{min}} = \frac{T_{\text{sys}}}{\sqrt{\Delta f \cdot \tau}} \tag{38}\)$ For channel bandwidth \(\Delta f = 4882.8125\text{ Hz}\) and integration time \(\tau = 0.200\text{ s}\)[cite: 1, 4]: $\(\sqrt{\Delta f \cdot \tau} = \sqrt{4882.8125 \times 0.200} = \sqrt{976.5625} = 31.25 \tag{39}\)$ $\(\Delta T_{\text{min}} = \frac{T_{\text{sys}}}{31.25} \tag{40}\)$ This integration yields a radiometric processing gain of[cite: 1]: $\(G_{\text{rad}} = 10 \log_{10}(31.25) \approx 14.9485\text{ dB} \tag{41}\)$ Relative to single-sample instantaneous fluctuations, the total processing gain across the \(2 \times 10^6\) raw samples ingested during each \(200\text{ ms}\) interval exceeds \(30\text{ dB}\)[cite: 1].

    3.4.3. Normalized Complex Coherence (\(\gamma[m]\)) and Magnitude dB Conversion¶

    The normalized complex coherence coefficient \(\gamma[m] \in [0, 1]\) measures the degree of correlation between the two antenna signals at each channel[cite: 1, 4]: $\(\gamma[m] = \frac{\big\vert{} \langle S_{12}[m] \rangle \big\vert{}}{\sqrt{\langle S_{11}[m] \rangle \cdot \langle S_{22}[m] \rangle}} \tag{42}\)$ * \(\gamma[m] = 1.0\): Fully coherent signal (ideal point source or common benchtop tone/noise source)[cite: 1, 7, 14]. * \(\gamma[m] = 0.0\): Completely uncorrelated signals (independent receiver thermal noise)[cite: 1, 7].

    For visualization and data streaming, the cross-power magnitude is converted to a logarithmic decibel scale[cite: 1, 4]: $\(P_{\text{dB}}[m] = 10 \log_{10}\Big( \big\vert{} \langle S_{12}[m] \rangle \big\vert{} + \epsilon \Big) \tag{43}\)$ where \(\epsilon = 1.0 \times 10^{-12}\) is a small positive constant preventing numeric underflow and \(-\infty\) evaluation during zero-signal conditions[cite: 1, 4, 10]. Finally, the array undergoes an in-place cyclic shift (std::rotate or block swap) swapping bins \([0, 1023]\) and \([1024, 2047]\), aligning the physical center frequency (\(35.0\text{ MHz}\)) to array index 1024[cite: 1, 4, 10].

    4. High-Throughput DSP Architecture & Memory Management¶

    4.1. Zero-Allocation Hot-Loop Philosophy¶

    At an ingest rate of \(10.0\text{ MSPS}\) per channel, the DSP pipeline processes one 1024-sample hop every \(102.4\ \mu\text{s}\)[cite: 1, 4]. Under this timing budget, invoking operating system heap management routines (malloc, free, new, realloc, or dynamically growing std::vector) in the execution loop is strictly prohibited[cite: 1, 10]. Dynamic memory management introduces lock contention and indeterminate latency spikes through kernel page allocation and heap consolidation, which risk overflowing the POSIX shared memory ring buffers[cite: 1, 10].

    All required computational buffers—including the sliding window memory, window weight vectors, FFTW input/output structures, and accumulator arrays—are pre-allocated once during class instantiation within the FXCorrelator constructor[cite: 1, 10]. The inner processing loop operates strictly on static memory locations, maintaining deterministic instruction timings[cite: 1, 10].

    4.2. 50% Overlapping Sliding Window Buffer Mechanics¶

    To sustain a continuous 50% overlap without re-reading samples from shared memory, each receiver channel maintains an internal scratch buffer of size \(N = 2048\) samples (ComplexSample)[cite: 1, 10]:

    4.3. ARMv8-A Cortex-A76 SIMD Optimization & FFTW3 Execution Engine¶

    The Raspberry Pi 5 is powered by a quad-core ARM Cortex-A76 processor operating at \(2.4\text{ GHz}\), supporting the ARMv8-A architecture with 128-bit NEON Advanced SIMD vector units[cite: 1, 10]. The DSP pipeline leverages these hardware capabilities through several design choices[cite: 1, 10]: * 64-Byte Cacheline Alignment: All dynamic FFT arrays are allocated using fftwf_alloc_complex(N), ensuring that base memory addresses align with 64-byte boundaries[cite: 1, 10]. This prevents unaligned memory penalties during 128-bit SIMD vector loads (VLD1.32) and stores (VST1.32)[cite: 1]. * Execution Plans (FFTW_MEASURE): FFT plans (fftwf_plan_dft_1d) are created during initialization using the FFTW_MEASURE flag[cite: 1, 10]. FFTW benchmarks multiple execution strategies directly on the Cortex-A76 pipeline, selecting optimal butterfly factorization trees tailored to the CPU's cache hierarchy before processing real data[cite: 1, 10]. * Contiguous Cartesian Vectorization: Arrays for real and imaginary components are organized contiguously in memory[cite: 1, 10]. When compiled with -O3 -march=native, the Cartesian cross-multiplication loops are automatically vectorized into NEON SIMD instructions, processing four single-precision floating-point values per instruction cycle[cite: 1, 10].

    4.4. High-Performance Non-Blocking POSIX UDP Telemetry Engine¶

    The telemetry engine streams integrated power spectra across the local network to remote display hosts without introducing stalls into the primary DSP thread[cite: 1, 4, 10].

    The network packet layout uses strict single-byte structure packing (#pragma pack(push, 1)), avoiding compiler-inserted padding bytes across hardware platforms[cite: 1, 10]:

    #pragma pack(push, 1)

    char     magic[4];            // 4 Bytes: Ident identifier string "SOLR"
    uint64_t frame_id;            // 8 Bytes: Monotonically increasing frame sequence
    uint64_t timestamp_us;        // 8 Bytes: Microseconds since Unix epoch (UTC)
    uint32_t integration_frames;  // 4 Bytes: Constant value = 1953
    uint32_t payload_flags;       // 4 Bytes: Bitmask (Bit 0: dB Power Spectrum)

#pragma pack(pop)

    The datagram payload consists of \(N = 2048\) single-precision floating-point values (\(2048 \times 4\text{ bytes} = 8192\text{ bytes}\)) representing the centered decibel power spectrum[cite: 1, 10]. The total datagram size is: $\(\text{Packet Size} = 32\text{ bytes (Header)} + 8192\text{ bytes (Payload)} = 8224\text{ bytes} \tag{44}\)$

    Key socket configuration details include[cite: 1, 10]: * Non-Blocking Operation: The socket descriptor is configured with the O_NONBLOCK file status flag via fcntl()[cite: 1, 10]. If network buffers become saturated, calls to sendto() return immediately with error code EWOULDBLOCK or EAGAIN rather than blocking the DSP thread[cite: 1, 10]. * Kernel Socket Buffer Expansion: The kernel transmit buffer (SO_SNDBUF) is set to \(1048576\text{ bytes}\) (\(1\text{ MB}\)) via setsockopt(), absorbing transient network stack delays without dropping frames[cite: 1, 10]. * Local MTU Handling: When traversing standard Ethernet networks with an MTU of 1500 bytes, IP fragmentation splits each 8224-byte packet into six fragments[cite: 1]. On gigabit LANs with standard switches, modern network stacks reassemble these fragments with packet drop rates below \(0.001\%\)[cite: 1]. Jumbo frame configurations (MTU 9000 bytes) can also be used to transmit the entire datagram within a single unfragmented Ethernet frame[cite: 1].

    5.3. Real-Time Correlation Execution Loop (process_cycle())¶

    The implementation of the correlation inner loop in correlator.cpp avoids heap allocations and optimizes SIMD execution[cite: 1, 10]:

    bool FXCorrelator::process_hop(SharedMemoryRingBuffer& rb1, 
                               SharedMemoryRingBuffer& rb2) 
{
    // 1. Shift upper half of sliding scratch buffers to lower half
    std::memcpy(&slide_ch1_[0], &slide_ch1_[HOP_SIZE], HOP_SIZE * sizeof(ComplexSample));
    std::memcpy(&slide_ch2_[0], &slide_ch2_[HOP_SIZE], HOP_SIZE * sizeof(ComplexSample));

    // 2. Ingest 1024 new samples from shared memory ring buffers
    while (rb1.available_read() < HOP_SIZE || rb2.available_read() < HOP_SIZE) {
        if (!g_running) return false;
        std::this_thread::yield();
    }
    rb1.read(&slide_ch1_[HOP_SIZE], HOP_SIZE);
    rb2.read(&slide_ch2_[HOP_SIZE], HOP_SIZE);

    // 3. F-Engine: Apply pre-computed Hanning window & convert int16 to float
    for (uint32_t n = 0; n < FFT_SIZE; ++n) {
        const float w = hanning_window_[n];
        fft_in_ch1_[n][0] = static_cast<float>(slide_ch1_[n].i) * w;
        fft_in_ch1_[n][1] = static_cast<float>(slide_ch1_[n].q) * w;
        fft_in_ch2_[n][0] = static_cast<float>(slide_ch2_[n].i) * w;
        fft_in_ch2_[n][1] = static_cast<float>(slide_ch2_[n].q) * w;
    }

    // 4. Execute 1D Forward FFTs via FFTW3 NEON kernels
    fftwf_execute(fft_plan_ch1_);
    fftwf_execute(fft_plan_ch2_);

    // 5. X-Engine: Cartesian Cross-Multiplication & Auto-Power Accumulation
    for (uint32_t m = 0; m < FFT_SIZE; ++m) {
        const float r1 = fft_out_ch1_[m][0];
        const float i1 = fft_out_ch1_[m][1];
        const float r2 = fft_out_ch2_[m][0];
        const float i2 = fft_out_ch2_[m][1];

        // Complex cross product: S12 = X1 * conj(X2)
        const float cross_r = r1 * r2 + i1 * i2;
        const float cross_i = i1 * r2 - r1 * i2;

        accum_cross_real_[m] += cross_r;
        accum_cross_imag_[m] += cross_i;
        accum_auto_ch1_[m]   += (r1 * r1 + i1 * i1);
        accum_auto_ch2_[m]   += (r2 * r2 + i2 * i2);
    }

    ++frames_accumulated_;
    return true;
}

bool FXCorrelator::finalize_integration(std::vector<float>& spectrum_db_out) {
    if (frames_accumulated_ < INTEGRATION_FRAMES) return false;

    const float norm_factor = 1.0f / (static_cast<float>(frames_accumulated_) * WINDOW_S2);

    for (uint32_t m = 0; m < FFT_SIZE; ++m) {
        const float cr = accum_cross_real_[m] * norm_factor;
        const float ci = accum_cross_imag_[m] * norm_factor;
        const float magnitude = std::sqrt(cr * cr + ci * ci);

        // Convert magnitude to logarithmic decibels
        uncentered_db_[m] = 10.0f * std::log10(magnitude + DYNAMIC_RANGE_EPS);
    }

    // Two-sided to centered frequency shifting (FFT Shift)
    // Swap lower half [0, 1023] and upper half [1024, 2047]
    const uint32_t half = FFT_SIZE / 2;
    std::memcpy(&spectrum_db_out[0],    &uncentered_db_[half], half * sizeof(float));
    std::memcpy(&spectrum_db_out[half], &uncentered_db_[0],    half * sizeof(float));

    // Reset accumulators for next integration cycle
    std::fill(accum_cross_real_.begin(), accum_cross_real_.end(), 0.0f);
    std::fill(accum_cross_imag_.begin(), accum_cross_imag_.end(), 0.0f);
    std::fill(accum_auto_ch1_.begin(),   accum_auto_ch1_.end(),   0.0f);
    std::fill(accum_auto_ch2_.begin(),   accum_auto_ch2_.end(),   0.0f);
    frames_accumulated_ = 0;

    return true;
}

    To prevent shared memory leaks and driver deadlocks upon termination, main.cpp installs POSIX signal handlers for SIGINT and SIGTERM[cite: 1, 10]: 1. Signal Interception: The signal handler toggles an atomic flag: 

    std::atomic<bool> g_running{true};
    2. Loop Termination: The primary DSP execution loop detects g_running == false and exits cleanly[cite: 1, 10]. 3. Child Process Termination: The parent process issues kill(pid, SIGTERM) to both SDRplay producer processes and awaits process termination using waitpid()[cite: 1, 10]. 4.     6. Experimental Verification & Benchmarking¶

    6.1. Multi-Stage Verification Methodology¶

    System qualification uses a four-stage verification methodology[cite: 1]: * Stage 0: Handover Verification: Validates that Module 2 achieves sample lock (\(k = 0\)) and maintains PNR \(\ge 40\text{ dB}\) before activating the correlator[cite: 1, 2]. * Stage 1: Synthetic CW Tone Test: Injecting a synthetic \(+1.0\text{ MHz}\) complex exponential (\(x[n] = e^{j 2\pi (1.0/10.0) n}\)) into both channels tests spectral bin localization[cite: 1, 4, 7, 14]. For \(f_{\text{offset}} = +1.0\text{ MHz}\), \(f_s = 10.0\text{ MSPS}\), and \(N = 2048\), the target bin index evaluates to[cite: 4, 7, 14]: $\(\text{bin}_{\text{peak}} = 1024 + \frac{1.0\text{ MHz}}{10.0\text{ MHz} / 2048} = 1024 + 204.8 \approx 1229 \tag{45}\)$ The peak must localize at bin 1229 with coherence \(\gamma \ge 0.99\)[cite: 1, 4, 7, 14]. * Stage 2: Independent Noise Suppression Test: Independent pseudo-random Gaussian noise streams are injected into Channel 1 and Channel 2[cite: 1, 4, 7, 14]. The integrated cross-power must decrease relative to the auto-power by a factor scaling as \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1953}} \approx \frac{1}{44.19}\) (an attenuation of \(\approx 16.45\text{ dB}\))[cite: 1, 4, 7, 14]. * Stage 3: Zero-Baseline Hardware Benchtop Run: Common avalanche noise is fed through a 1:2 resistive splitter into both receivers[cite: 1, 7, 11, 14]. The measured coherence across the active passband (\(31 - 39\text{ MHz}\)) must satisfy \(\gamma(f) \ge 0.95\), while the cross-power phase \(\Delta\phi(f)\) must remain flat within \(\pm 2^\circ\)[cite: 1, 4, 14]. * Stage 4: 60-Second Real-Time Telemetry Benchmark: The full system runs continuously for 60 seconds, streaming telemetry to viewer.py[cite: 1, 2, 10]. Verification requires zero dropped buffers, steady CPU load, and stable transmission cadences (\(200\text{ ms} \pm 2\text{ ms}\))[cite: 1, 4].
